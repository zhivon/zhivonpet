Three Steps at the Bench

  1. Soften. Submerge the splint sheet in warm water at approximately 70 °C. The low-temperature thermoplastic polymer turns pliable within moments.
  2. Shape. Lift the sheet from the water and conform it directly over the patient's limb or body region. Ventilation holes across the board keep the contact surface open to air.
  3. Set. Allow roughly five minutes of air cooling; the polymer locks into its new contour. Should a minor adjustment be needed later, gentle re-warming softens just the area that needs it, so the fit can be refined at the bench.

Where the Splint Fits In

ZHIVON™ splints are an auxiliary support tool, not a medical device. Clinics reach for them alongside standard care when a canine or feline patient is working through a leg muscle strain, a soft-tissue bruise, or the healing phase following a fracture. The same principle extends to gentle support of the spine, ribcage, or tail. The Professional series is the recommended choice whenever the fitting is performed by a licensed veterinarian in a clinical setting.

Why a Low-Temperature Thermoplastic Polymer?

The material was chosen for three practical reasons a working vet notices immediately. It conforms closely to irregular limb and body contours, so the fit follows the animal rather than forcing the animal into a rigid mould. The cured sheet is light enough that a small patient carries it without added strain. And the built-in ventilation holes let air reach the skin — a detail that matters across the full wearing period. Together these traits make the splint a comfortable, adjustable, bench-friendly option for rehabilitation support.
